Exact-room, SCB file-exchange, contract, and roommate PDPA update

Last revised: 2026-07-10

The accepted pilot now processes additional personal and operational data beyond the earlier application/slip design:

  • exact building/floor/room assignment;
  • roommate invitation, group membership, leader, and confirmation events;
  • shared versus whole-room occupancy;
  • resident-specific pricing and amount overrides;
  • SCB Excel export rows;
  • returned multipage QR payment-form PDFs;
  • SCB payment-result reports and transaction references;
  • annual contract snapshots and signed scans/photos;
  • separate key-handover signatures;
  • university handoff and correction files.

Required privacy controls

  • Roommate invitations must reveal only the minimum profile information needed to recognize the invited person.
  • A roommate may see group-completion status but not unnecessary details of the other resident’s bank transaction or payment evidence.
  • Exact room assignment should be visible only to the resident/group and authorized staff; public availability screens must not reveal occupant names.
  • SCB XLSX, returned PDFs, result reports, contracts, scans, and handoff files are private files.
  • Store original imported files, checksums, mappings, access events, and retention status.
  • Do not rely on Ref.1/room number as an access secret.
  • PDF page extraction and matching must run in authorized backend processing; unmatched page text is restricted to finance staff.
  • Manual payment records must store only the necessary bank reference and staff note, not an unrestricted full bank statement.
  • Amount overrides, manual reservations/payments, roommate replacements, contract corrections, and university correction exports require purpose, actor, reason, and audit history.
  • Signed contract scans/photos and key-handover records require a retention and secure-destruction policy.
  • Public room plans must not expose current occupant identity or sensitive notes.
  • Correction exports must preserve legal/accounting history while limiting duplicate distribution of personal data.

Privacy notice additions

Applicants should be told that the system:

  • shares required billing fields with the existing SCB/internal Bill Payment workflow;
  • stores returned official payment-form pages and payment-result status;
  • processes roommate-group data;
  • generates paper contracts and stores signed digital copies;
  • sends confirmed/corrected records to official university workflows;
  • may retain audit history even when an operational record is superseded.

Data minimization decisions

  • ID in the SCB import remains blank under the current template.
  • Use only required Payer Name, room/action references, amount, issue date, email, alert, and remark fields.
  • Do not expose one roommate’s transaction details to the other.
  • Store normalized resident-specific text needed for PDF matching, but avoid retaining unnecessary extracted page content outside the protected document record.

Purpose

This page records Thai legal notes for the KKU Affiliated Dorm Application & Reservation System under the Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019). It is a project requirement reference, not formal legal advice.

The goal is to map the real Thai PDPA requirements to system design decisions for applicant data, student records, payment slips, uploaded documents, printed contracts and generated contract artifacts, staff verification, export/reporting, and audit logs.

Primary legal references

  • พระราชบัญญัติคุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล พ.ศ. 2562, Royal Gazette publication dated 27 May 2019.
  • Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019), full enforcement from 1 June 2022 after extensions and establishment of the Personal Data Protection Committee.
  • Subordinate PDPA regulations/guidelines on security measures, personal data breach notification, data subject requests, DPO criteria, and cross-border transfer should be checked again before any real pilot.
  • Working legal summary checked on 7 July 2026: DLA Piper Data Protection Laws of the World — Thailand, last modified 14 February 2026.

Why PDPA applies to this system

The system collects, uses, stores, displays, verifies, exports, and may disclose information that can identify students or applicants directly or indirectly. Examples:

  • Full name
  • Student ID
  • Phone number
  • Email
  • Faculty / major / year level
  • Applicant type
  • Application status
  • Reservation status
  • Payment status
  • Payment slips
  • Uploaded applicant documents
  • Staff notes and rejection reasons
  • Exported reports
  • Generated contracts, printed copies, signatures, contract numbers, signing dates, and paper-storage references

These are personal data because they relate to an identifiable person. Payment slips and uploaded documents are especially risky because they may contain bank account information, payment references, names, timestamps, QR/payment metadata, or other personal identifiers.

Key Thai PDPA legal points mapped to this project

1. Definition of personal data — PDPA Section 6

Personal data means data relating to a person that can identify that person, directly or indirectly, excluding data of deceased persons.

Project implication:

  • Student records, application data, reservation records, and payment slips must be treated as personal data.
  • Do not use real student data in demo screenshots unless anonymized.

2. Data Controller / Data Processor — PDPA Section 6

Data Controller determines the purpose and manner of personal data processing. Data Processor processes data on behalf of or according to the instruction of the Data Controller.

Project implication:

  • Need to confirm who is the Data Controller: likely KKU / Dorm Service Division / responsible dorm unit, not the student project team.
  • If an external hosting provider, cloud storage provider, or third-party service processes personal data for the system, it may be a Data Processor and should have an agreement or clear control terms.
  • If the system is only self-hosted on university infrastructure, clarify which university unit owns the data and system operation.

3. Collection limitation / data minimization — PDPA Section 22

The system should collect personal data only as necessary for lawful purposes.

Project implication:

  • Every application field should have a purpose.
  • Avoid optional fields that are not needed for dorm application, reservation, payment verification, or official export.
  • Do not collect national ID, health data, religion, disability status, or other high-risk fields unless stakeholders confirm a clear legal/operational need.

4. Privacy notice before or at collection — PDPA Section 23

The Data Controller must notify the data subject before or at the time of personal data collection. The notice should explain key details such as purpose, retention, disclosure, contact channel, and rights.

Project implication:

  • Add a privacy notice on the applicant form before submission.
  • The notice should be available by URL/QR/link and written in clear Thai language.
  • The notice should explain:
    • what data is collected;
    • why the data is collected;
    • whether the applicant must provide the data;
    • who can access the data;
    • whether data is exported to existing official workflows;
    • how long data is retained;
    • how applicants can contact the responsible unit;
    • data subject rights.

5. Lawful basis and consent — PDPA Sections 19, 24, 27

PDPA does not mean every data item always needs consent. Consent is one legal basis, but other legal bases may apply, such as contract/pre-contract necessity, legal obligation, public task, legitimate interest, or vital interest depending on the real workflow.

Project implication:

  • For core dorm application/reservation data, likely legal bases to confirm are:
    • necessary for application/reservation process or contract-like dorm service process;
    • necessary for official university/dorm operation;
    • legal obligation or public task where university rules require records;
    • consent only for optional or non-essential processing.
  • Do not rely on one generic consent checkbox for everything.
  • Map each data category to a purpose and legal basis.

6. Sensitive personal data — PDPA Section 26

Sensitive personal data includes race, ethnicity, political opinion, religious/philosophical belief, sexual behavior, criminal record, health, disability, labour union data, genetic data, biometric data, or other similarly sensitive data.

Project implication:

  • Avoid collecting sensitive data in the core product release.
  • If the dorm needs disability, health, medical, or special accommodation information, treat it as sensitive personal data and require special review with university legal/DPO.
  • Do not include sensitive data in normal export unless strictly necessary and approved.

7. Use/disclosure limitation — PDPA Section 27

Personal data should not be used or disclosed beyond the notified purpose unless a lawful basis or exception applies.

Project implication:

  • Staff should access applicant data only for application, verification, reservation, report, or official processing.
  • Exported reports should go only to authorized staff and official workflows.
  • Do not reuse applicant data for unrelated analytics, marketing, or public presentation.

8. Security measures — PDPA Section 37 and Security Measures Notification B.E. 2565

The Data Controller must provide appropriate security measures to protect personal data from loss, unauthorized or unlawful access, use, alteration, or disclosure. Security measures should include organizational, technical, and physical measures and should be reviewed periodically.

Project implication:

  • Role-based access control is required.
  • Staff permissions should be scoped by role and dorm/campaign.
  • QR management, bank-statement access, payment verification, and reservation approval must be separate permissions.
  • Only selected admin roles may change the shared QR.
  • Only authorized payment-verification staff may access bank-statement or matched-transaction information.
  • Payment slips and documents must be private by default.
  • Do not expose raw SeaweedFS, Filer, or S3 gateway object URLs publicly.
  • Serve files through backend-authorized endpoints or limited-access links.
  • Log sensitive actions such as viewing slips, viewing matched-transaction details, verifying payment, rejecting payment, changing the shared QR, approving reservations, generating/printing/reprinting contracts, recording physical signature receipt, cancelling reservations, changing quota, and exporting reports.
  • Restrict access to generated contract artifacts and printed-paper storage because contracts contain applicant and financial/reservation data.
  • Require a reason for contract reprints and preserve previous print history.
  • Do not ingest or retain the complete bank statement in the MVP by default. Store only the minimum matched-transaction metadata needed for verification, duplicate prevention, dispute handling, and audit.
  • Add periodic review of access permissions and security settings before pilot.

9. Personal data breach notification — PDPA Section 37 and Breach Notification Regulation B.E. 2565

If a personal data breach occurs, the Data Controller must assess risk and notify the PDPC without undue delay, where feasible within 72 hours after becoming aware. If the breach is high risk to rights and freedoms of individuals, affected data subjects must also be notified with remedial measures.

Project implication:

  • Add an incident response checklist before real pilot.
  • Log access and export events so a breach can be investigated.
  • Prepare breach severity levels and notification owner.
  • Store DPO/responsible unit contact information in system operation docs.

10. Deletion, destruction, anonymization, and retention — PDPA Sections 33 and 37

The system should support deletion, destruction, or anonymization when the retention period ends, data is no longer necessary, or a valid request applies, subject to legal/official record exceptions.

Project implication:

  • Need a retention policy for applications, slips, generated contract artifacts, signed paper contracts, exported reports, and audit logs.
  • Suggested placeholder before stakeholder/legal confirmation:
    • active campaign data: keep during application/reservation process;
    • approved reservation records: keep as required by official dorm/university record rules;
    • rejected/cancelled applications and slips: define shorter retention if not needed;
    • audit logs: keep long enough for dispute, accountability, and official review.
  • Do not delete official records automatically until university record-retention rules are confirmed.

11. Record of processing activities — PDPA Section 39

Data Controllers must maintain records that help demonstrate and verify personal data processing activities.

Project implication:

  • Keep a data inventory for this system.
  • Record categories of personal data, purposes, legal bases, retention period, recipients, access roles, security measures, and transfer/export paths.
  • The system's audit log supports operational accountability but does not fully replace the legal processing record.

12. Data Processor duties — PDPA Section 40

Data Processors must process data according to the Data Controller's instructions, provide security measures, notify the Data Controller of breaches, and keep processing records as required.

Project implication:

  • If a third-party provider is used for hosting, storage, email/SMS, analytics, or future payment integration, confirm processor/controller role.
  • For the manual MVP, confirm who owns the destination bank account, who controls bank-statement access, and whether the dorm system stores any matched transaction details.
  • Avoid adding external analytics or third-party tracking in core product release unless reviewed.

13. DPO requirement — PDPA Section 41

A DPO may be required for public authorities, large-scale regular monitoring, or core activities involving sensitive personal data, depending on criteria and subordinate regulations.

Project implication:

  • Confirm whether KKU already has a university DPO or PDPA contact.
  • Add DPO/responsible office contact to privacy notice before any real pilot.
  • The student project team should not invent the official DPO contact.

14. Data subject rights — PDPA Chapter on data subject rights

Data subjects have rights such as access/copy, correction, objection, deletion/destruction/anonymization, restriction, portability where applicable, withdrawal of consent where consent is used, and complaint.

Project implication:

  • Add a support channel for applicants to ask for correction or privacy-related requests.
  • Staff should be able to correct applicant data with audit log.
  • Do not build full self-service rights workflow in core product release unless required; but define manual handling for pilot.

Concrete system requirements to add or keep

  • Privacy notice on applicant form before submission.
  • Data-field inventory and purpose mapping.
  • Legal-basis mapping by data category.
  • Role-based access control by user role and dorm/campaign scope.
  • Separate permissions for QR administration, bank-statement access, payment verification, reservation confirmation, contract-template management, contract generation/printing, and signed-paper receipt.
  • Minimize matched-transaction metadata and avoid storing a complete bank statement unless officially required.
  • Private file storage; no public raw slip/document URLs.
  • Backend-authorized file access.
  • Audit log for sensitive actions.
  • Export permission only for authorized staff.
  • Export minimization: include only fields needed by official workflow.
  • Retention/deletion policy before real pilot.
  • Breach response checklist before real pilot.
  • DPO/responsible unit contact placeholder before real pilot.
  • Avoid sensitive personal data in core product release unless approved.

Open legal questions for stakeholders / university legal or DPO

  • Who is the official Data Controller for this system?
  • Is the student project team only developer/operator during prototype, or will it handle real personal data?
  • Which lawful basis should be used for application, reservation, payment verification, contract generation/physical signing, report export, and audit log?
  • What is the official retention period for applications, payment slips, generated contract artifacts, signed paper contracts, and approved reservation records?
  • Will the pilot use real student data or mock/anonymized data?
  • Will any sensitive data be collected, such as disability, health, or special accommodation needs?
  • Which exact staff roles can view slips, access bank-statement information, verify payments, export reports, and change reservation status?
  • Which selected admin roles can change the shared QR and destination-account instructions?
  • Is access to the existing bank statement covered by current dorm/university policy, and what transaction metadata may the new system retain?
  • Is there an existing KKU privacy notice or PDPA policy that this system must follow?

Suggested PRD wording

The system processes personal data under Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 because it stores and uses student/applicant information, application records, payment slips, reservation status, generated contract data, and records of physical signing. The system must collect only necessary data, provide a clear privacy notice before or at collection, restrict access by role and dorm/campaign scope, store payment slips and contract artifacts privately, protect signed paper contracts physically, avoid exposing raw storage URLs, log sensitive staff actions, limit export to authorized official workflows, define digital and paper retention/deletion rules before real pilot use, and prepare a breach-response process that supports PDPC notification within 72 hours where legally required.

Practical project conclusion

For this project, PDPA should be treated as a non-functional requirement and operational constraint. The core prototype can use mock/anonymized data. If the project moves to real pilot use, privacy notice, role permissions, audit log, retention policy, breach response, and official DPO/legal review become required readiness items.

Optional applicant identity linking and printed-contract implications

Last revised (GMT+7): 2026-07-10 14:40

The verified personal-account, optional KKU SSO linking, and printed-contract flow add personal-data processing that must be included in the data inventory, privacy notice, access design, digital/paper retention policy, and audit plan.

Additional data categories:

  • personal email used for the applicant account;
  • email-verification state and verification events;
  • phone number used for applicant contact or account recovery;
  • password hash and credential-security metadata, never the plaintext password;
  • KKU provider subject and student ID after linking;
  • only the normalized KKU claims needed for identity, eligibility, or official workflow;
  • optional identity-link status, conflict reason, and review decision;
  • contract template/version, generated data snapshot, contract number, print/reprint events, signing date, receiving staff, and paper-storage reference.

Required controls:

  • Explain at registration that a verified personal account supports the complete pilot workflow and that KKU SSO is optional.
  • Use personal email only for account access, application communication, recovery, and other notified purposes.
  • Store passwords only as strong salted hashes and store verification/reset tokens in a protected, expiring form.
  • Do not retain raw SSO access tokens, ID tokens, or complete claim payloads longer than required for authentication and approved audit needs.
  • Do not auto-merge applicants based only on matching name, email, phone, or claimed student ID.
  • Restrict identity-conflict details and KKU claims to staff with dedicated review permission.
  • Log link request, successful link, rejected link, manual correction, and unlink events.
  • Log contract generation, template version, print/reprint, correction, cancellation, physical-signature receipt, and paper-storage reference.
  • Define how personal-email login is retained, disabled, or kept as recovery after KKU SSO linking; communicate that behavior in the privacy notice and account settings.
  • Provide an audited correction route when KKU claims and application data differ.
  • Avoid displaying unnecessary KKU claims or identity-conflict details in normal payment and reservation exports.

Readiness questions before a real pilot:

  • If production KKU SSO is later enabled, what claims are officially supplied and which may be retained?
  • Which role reviews optional identity-link mismatches?
  • What is the official contract template/version approval process and paper retention period?
  • Which roles may generate, print/reprint, receive, store, or dispose of paper contracts?
  • Must personal-email login remain usable after optional linking, or should it become recovery-only?
